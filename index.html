Why 30 W is hardly faster than 20 W

Fast charging only happens in the constant-current stage of the lithium-ion charge cycle. The phone, not the charger, decides how much current it accepts, and an iPhone tops out a little above 20 W for a few minutes before heat and cell voltage pull it down. Past about 80% it switches to a slow constant-voltage finish no matter what is plugged in.

The charger and phone agree on voltage through USB Power Delivery, which needs a USB-C end on the charger side. On a Lightning iPhone that means a USB-C to Lightning cable — an anker powerline usb-c to lightning or Apple's own both work; the old USB-A to Lightning lead caps you at the 12 W row above.

Heat matters more than wattage: a phone charging under a pillow or in a hot car will throttle well below these numbers (Battery University on charging temperature).
